01The answer on one page

One small rule turned a timing change into a trap.

What happened

4.2 cut the ping wait from 90 to 60 seconds. Missed pings jumped from 2% to 23%. The code scores a miss exactly like a "no" (−0.12), and only a "yes" earns points back (+0.08). Four responders sank to a score of zero, and Dispatch stopped asking them: 51 pings a week fell to 6.

Why nobody caught it

The headline metric recovered. Acceptance was back to 75% by September because the four weren't being asked any more, and the other twelve quietly absorbed their work. No brief, no experiment, no owner watching: Priya left on 21 Aug and Wen was away until the 24th.

What it cost

At the peak, 1 in 5 callouts found nobody (19%, up from about 5%). Support tickets went up 4×. A quarter of the roster sat idle while the rest carried 27% more, and Rook bills per active responder.

What I'd do

Keep the 60-second wait and the proximity change. Stop counting a miss as a no, and give quiet responders a way back with a 5-day "still available?" check-in. Show handlers why the work is lopsided. Make "system health" a release gate.

Decision needed

Approve a two-week staged rollout in two areas, starting with Eastgate, behind a flag, with guardrails at both ends and a rollback trigger. Engineering says the core change is small. The risk is in shipping it the way 4.2 shipped, not in the code.

03Why the dashboard looked fine

The average hid four people.

Acceptance rate: 75.8% → 54.3% → 75.2%. By early September the headline number was back to normal.

It recovered because the people who were missing pings stopped being asked. The system "healed" by quietly removing a quarter of its capacity.

Coverage came back by overloading the rest. The other twelve went from about 10 to 13 pings each a week. The Gale, in Eastgate, hit 15–20.

One more bad week for them and the same loop starts from the other end: tired responders miss pings too.

Lesson for how we measure: every success metric needs a guardrail on the distribution, not just the average. Nobody idle for 5+ days. Nobody far above their usual load.

04Root cause

A loop with no brake.

The warning signs were all on record.

Wen flagged the missing brake in 2019. On 14 Aug, two days after release, Marcus asked whether the change was meant to hit responders who'd been turning jobs down. Nobody answered. Missed and turned-down pings are stored separately, so the data to tell them apart was always there.

Not seasonal. Early August ran at 77.9% taken. The drop starts on release day, and missed pings begin on 12 Aug itself. Past turn-downs don't predict who went quiet. Misses after 4.2 do.

07Choosing the fix · same pings, different rules

One rule change does most of the work.

✕

Roll back 4.2

Proximity weighting was asked for by responders covering wide areas. A rollback swaps one angry group for another, and leaves the trap in the code.

✕

Add score recovery only

Too slow. The four end at 0.23–0.35 and still sit 10 minutes behind.

✓

A miss isn't a no

The four stay at 0.76–0.84, about 3 minutes behind. That's the lever. The replay understates it, because they'd also have got more pings.

✓

Plus a way back for those already stuck

On launch day the four are still at 0. Only the 5-day check-in gets them asked again (Module 5 finding).

08The fix, live · try it

Built around Mite, seen through Kip's console.

1 · A miss isn't a no

Still recorded, no longer punished.

2 · "Still available?"

After 3 misses in a row or 5 quiet days. A yes resets to neutral, 0.50. A "not tonight" costs nothing.

3 · Kip sees why

Usual pings, a reason, a 5-day warning and a score line on each card.

4 · Quick no, faster help

A no within 40s earns +0.02, capped at 0.50. Moved to "Next": it speeds up the system, it doesn't fix Mite.

  1. Click Launch day, then Today (4.2). Send callouts: Mite stays at 0.00.
  2. Switch to With the fix. Skip to Thursday: Kip's card warns, and Mite's phone asks.
  3. Tap Yes, I'm here, then take the next callouts. Watch Mite's score line climb.

North star

Share of callouts with a yes within X minutes. Until answer time is tracked: callouts nobody took, back to ≤ 5.5%.

Guardrails, both ends

No available responder idle for 5+ days. Nobody above 1.5× their usual pings for a week.

Rollback trigger (proposed)

Callouts nobody took above 8% for 3 days in a test area, or a guardrail broken for a week: the flag goes off.
